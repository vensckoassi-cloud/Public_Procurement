# Answer key — Mankiw, *Macroeconomics*, Ch. 4 (The Monetary System)

Quiz id `MANKIW-CH4` · 20 questions · 43 points · types: 7 MCQ, 4 true/false, 2 select-all, 5 numeric, 1 short answer, 1 essay

**Q01** (mcq, 2 pt) A student keeps part of this month's wage in cash to buy textbooks next month. Which function of money does this illustrate?
- A. Medium of exchange
- B. Unit of account
- C. Store of value
- D. Standard of deferred payment

*Answer:* C  
*Explanation:* Money as a store of value transfers purchasing power from the present to the future.

**Q02** (mcq, 2 pt) Money that has no intrinsic value and is accepted because the government decrees it is called:
- A. Commodity money
- B. Fiat money
- C. Near money
- D. Gold-backed money

*Answer:* B  
*Explanation:* Fiat money is established by government decree; commodity money (e.g. gold) has intrinsic value.

**Q03** (tf, 1 pt) Under 100-percent-reserve banking, banks do not affect the money supply.

*Answer:* TRUE  
*Explanation:* Each deposit is fully held as reserves, so deposits simply replace currency one-for-one and M is unchanged.

**Q04** (mcq, 2 pt) In the model of the money supply, the monetary base $B$ is equal to:
- A. Currency plus demand deposits
- B. Currency plus bank reserves
- C. Bank reserves plus demand deposits
- D. Demand deposits only

*Answer:* B  
*Explanation:* B = C + R: the currency held by the public plus the reserves held by banks (high-powered money).

**Q05** (numeric, 2 pt) The currency–deposit ratio is $cr = 0.2$ and the reserve–deposit ratio is $rr = 0.1$. Compute the money multiplier $m$.

*Answer:* 4 (tolerance 0.01)  
*Explanation:* m = (cr + 1)/(cr + rr) = 1.2/0.3 = 4.

**Q06** (numeric, 3 pt) Suppose $cr = 0.25$, $rr = 0.15$ and the monetary base is $B = 800$ (billions). Compute the money supply $M$ (in billions).

*Answer:* 2500 (tolerance 1)  
*Explanation:* m = 1.25/0.40 = 3.125, so M = m × B = 3.125 × 800 = 2500.

**Q07** (mcq, 2 pt) Using $m = \frac{cr+1}{cr+rr}$ with $rr < 1$, an increase in the currency–deposit ratio $cr$ (other things equal):
- A. Raises the money multiplier
- B. Lowers the money multiplier
- C. Leaves the money multiplier unchanged
- D. Has an ambiguous effect on the money multiplier

*Answer:* B  
*Explanation:* dm/dcr = (rr − 1)/(cr + rr)^2 < 0: when people hold more currency, fewer reserves support deposit creation.

**Q08** (multi, 3 pt) Which of the following are instruments the Federal Reserve uses to influence the money supply or interest rates? (select all that apply)
- A. Open-market operations
- B. Reserve requirements
- C. Interest paid on reserves
- D. Setting the federal income tax rate
- E. Lending to banks at the discount rate

*Answer:* A,B,C,E  
*Explanation:* Tax rates are fiscal policy, set by Congress. The others are monetary policy tools.

**Q09** (tf, 1 pt) When the Fed buys government bonds from the public in an open-market operation, the monetary base falls.

*Answer:* FALSE  
*Explanation:* An open-market purchase pays for bonds with new money, so the monetary base rises.

**Q10** (numeric, 2 pt) A bank has total assets of 1,000 and bank capital of 50. What is its leverage ratio?

*Answer:* 20 (tolerance 0.01)  
*Explanation:* Leverage ratio = assets / bank capital = 1000 / 50 = 20.

**Q11** (numeric, 2 pt) A bank has a leverage ratio of 20. If the value of its assets falls by 2 percent, by what percentage does its bank capital fall? (enter a number of percent, e.g. 15)

*Answer:* 40 (tolerance 0.5)  
*Explanation:* Capital is 1/20 of assets, so a 2% fall in assets = 0.02 × 20 = 40% of capital. Leverage magnifies losses.

**Q12** (mcq, 2 pt) According to Mankiw's account of 1929–1933, the U.S. money supply fell by more than a quarter mainly because:
- A. The Fed sharply reduced the monetary base
- B. Bank failures raised both the currency–deposit and reserve–deposit ratios
- C. Congress abolished the gold standard
- D. Large gold discoveries reduced the value of money

*Answer:* B  
*Explanation:* The monetary base actually rose; bank failures made households hold more currency and banks hold more reserves, so the multiplier collapsed.

**Q13** (tf, 1 pt) After the 2008 financial crisis, the very large increase in the monetary base was matched by a roughly proportional increase in M2.

*Answer:* FALSE  
*Explanation:* Banks held most of the new base as excess reserves, so the reserve–deposit ratio rose, the multiplier fell and M2 grew far less than B.

**Q14** (multi, 3 pt) Which of the following are included in M2? (select all that apply)
- A. Currency
- B. Demand deposits
- C. Savings deposits
- D. Small time deposits
- E. Large time deposits
- F. U.S. Treasury bonds held by households

*Answer:* A,B,C,D  
*Explanation:* M2 = M1 plus savings deposits, small time deposits and retail money market funds. Large time deposits and Treasury bonds are not in M2.

**Q15** (short, 2 pt) When a central bank buys large quantities of long-term bonds and other assets to expand the monetary base beyond normal open-market operations, the policy is called ______.

*Answer:* quantitative easing / QE / quantitative easing qe / large-scale asset purchases / large scale asset purchases  
*Explanation:* Quantitative easing (QE), used by the Fed after 2008 and again in 2020.

**Q16** (mcq, 2 pt) If the Fed raises the interest rate it pays on bank reserves, other things equal, this tends to:
- A. Raise the reserve–deposit ratio and reduce the money supply
- B. Lower the reserve–deposit ratio and increase the money supply
- C. Leave the money supply unchanged
- D. Raise the currency–deposit ratio

*Answer:* A  
*Explanation:* Holding reserves becomes more attractive, so banks hold more reserves (rr rises), lowering the multiplier and M.

**Q17** (mcq, 2 pt) The main purpose of federal deposit insurance is to:
- A. Prevent bank runs by maintaining depositor confidence
- B. Raise the reserve–deposit ratio
- C. Finance the government budget deficit
- D. Fix the price of gold

*Answer:* A  
*Explanation:* Insured depositors have no reason to rush to withdraw, which prevents self-fulfilling bank runs.

**Q18** (numeric, 2 pt) In a fractional-reserve system where the public holds no currency and every bank keeps a reserve ratio of 0.2, a new deposit of 100 eventually supports a total money supply of how much?

*Answer:* 500 (tolerance 0.5)  
*Explanation:* With cr = 0, m = 1/rr = 1/0.2 = 5, so the 100 of reserves supports 5 × 100 = 500.

**Q19** (tf, 1 pt) If the currency–deposit ratio is zero, the money multiplier equals $1/rr$.

*Answer:* TRUE  
*Explanation:* m = (cr + 1)/(cr + rr) = 1/rr when cr = 0.

**Q20** (essay, 6 pt) Using the money-supply model $M = m \times B$ with $m = \frac{cr+1}{cr+rr}$, explain how a wave of bank runs can reduce the money supply even if the central bank holds the monetary base constant. What can policymakers do about it?

*Answer:* Runs raise cr (households switch deposits to currency) and rr (banks hold precautionary reserves); both lower m, so M falls with B fixed (cf. 1929–33). Remedies: deposit insurance, lender of last resort, expanding B to offset the fall in m.
